# 1♣–2♦ Multi and 1♣–2♥ Reverse Flannery

Research checked 9 October 2026. This reviews Muscat's direct jumps,
**1♣–2♦ = 4–7 with a weak six-card major** and **1♣–2♥ = 8–11 with
5+♠ and 4+♥**, against published agreements. It supplements the
[survey of direct 1m–2♥ ranges](1m-2h-strength-ranges.md).

## Direct 1♣–2♦ as a weak Multi

**The weak one-suited Multi response is documented, including in T-Walsh
systems.** Published ranges run from 0–7 to 6–9.

### Continuations

**Only Aggarwal documents a full continuation scheme.** Opener's 2♥ and 2♠
are pass-or-correct; over 2♠ a heart responder shows minimum or maximum,
or shortness. 2NT asks: 3♣ = maximum, 3♦ = minimum hearts, 3♥ = minimum
spades. [Article, p. 2](https://www.bridgewebs.com/dba/Newsletter_Dec2004.pdf#page=2).
Drijver–Schollaardt's card gives only “1C-2D: 2HS P/C 2N F1R”.
[WBF card, p. 2](https://www.jcbl.or.jp/Portals/0/pdf/nec/2005/DrijverSchollaardt.pdf#page=2).
The other cards above define the response but not the continuations.

### The case against weak jumps after T-Walsh

A weak one-suiter can already transfer and pass a nonforcing completion,
so some players argue the direct jumps should carry something else:
“This eliminates the need for weak jump shifts and strongly suggests
playing something artificial.”
[DavidKok, BBO Forums, April 2023, post #7](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/88493-wjs-sjs-solway-other/page__p__1053059).
This is a forum opinion. Molenaar–Verbeek's invitational 2♦ is the
closest published T-Walsh structure following this reasoning.

## Direct 1♣–2♥ as Reverse Flannery

**8–11 is well supported after 1♣.** Molenaar–Verbeek play 7–11 (T-Walsh),
Kokish–Kraft about 7–11 and Levin–Weinstein 9–11; details and links are in
the [strength-range survey](1m-2h-strength-ranges.md).

**Muscat's shape is wider than the T-Walsh precedents.** Molenaar–Verbeek
and Levin–Weinstein use exactly 5♠–4♥ for 1♣–2♥; Levin–Weinstein show
6♠–4♥ separately with 2♠.
[Levin–Weinstein, note 1](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/WeinsteinLevinWBF2022.pdf#page=3).
Bridge World Standard and Kokish–Kraft allow 5+♠–4+♥.

## Fit with Muscat — analysis

- **The pair matches published allocations.** Aggarwal pairs a weak Multi
  2♦ with Reverse Flannery; Levin–Weinstein put the weak branch at 2♦ and
  9–11 at 2♥. Muscat's 4–7 and 8–11 split leaves no gap or overlap.
- **The 4 HCP floor** mirrors the 1♠ response; 0–3 hands with a long major
  transfer and pass.
- **Undefined so far:** Multi continuations, which matter because 1♣
  includes 18–20 balanced; whether 2♥ is passable; how opener invites over
  2♥; and the meaning of a transfer followed by a rebid of the major, such
  as 1♣–1♦; 1♥–2♥, now that 4–7 one-suiters start with 2♦.
