# Continuations after a game-forcing minor response to 1♣

Research checked 10 October 2026. This note collects published continuations
after a **game-forcing minor-suit response** to a short, natural or Polish
1♣ (and clearly transferable 1♦ analogues). It is meant to inform
**1♣–2♣ = GF diamonds** and **1♣–2♠ = GF clubs**. Responder is unpassed
and uncontested unless a source says otherwise. It records evidence only and
does not change Muscat's system.

Ranges are quoted as printed. Polish text is quoted verbatim, with our
translation in brackets. Most cards define the response but not the
follow-ups, so every continuation found is listed, even single lines.

Sweep coverage: the 581 Transnational Swiss Cup card files on r.pzbs.pl from
2021–2024 (29 failed to parse), the NBB and Spanish cards used in
[1c-minor-responses.md](1c-minor-responses.md), Watson, Goetze, Toucan,
Fallenius–Welland, Bridge World Standard 2017, *Wspólny Język* 2005 (English)
and 2020 (Polish, pp. 25–59), and Jassem's *Polish Club 2010* web book.

## 1♣–2♣ as a GF diamond transfer

**The transfer write-ups say “complete unless unusual”. Opener's exceptions
are strong balanced (2NT), a 4-card diamond fit with shortness (new suit), or
a 5-card major. No source defines responder's second bid beyond “natural,
game-forcing”.**

### A club-raise analogue below GF: Goetze 1♣–1♠

Goetze's 1♠ shows “4 or more clubs without a 4 card major” and “a full 7
points”. Opener: “2♦/♥/♠ 16+, 4+ clubs, 3+ and values in suit bid”;
“3♦/♥/♠ 18+ with shortness in the suit bid”; “4♣ RKCB for clubs”. Over
opener's 2♣, “2♦ is an artificial game-force confirming clubs, generally not
interested in 3nt”, “2♥/♠ show stoppers”, and “other 3-level bids show
shortness”. “If opener pulls responder's 3NT to 4♣, it is RKCB, but if
opener bids 4♣ over responder's shortness-showing bid, it merely denies a
stopper and is nonforcing.”
[pp. 9–10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=9).

## Relays after a minor response

- **Goodman–Short:** after 1♣–2♣ (GF raise), opener's 2♦ = 4+♣ and
  responder's **2♥ = GF relay** (above). The relay answers are not given.
- **Goded–Goded, 1♦–2♣ (“F1”, not GF):** “1♦-2♣-2♦ weak bal or nat,
  1♦-2♣-2♦-2♥ asks / Then 2♠ 5+♦, 2NT bal, 3♣ bal nat, 3♦ solid.”
  [p. 2](https://www.aebridge.com/sites/default/files/2024-05/Goded-Goded2024.pdf#page=2).
- **Watson, unbalanced 1♦:** 1♦–1NT = “Artificial GF with slam interest.
  15+ HCP. Opener rebids naturally with 2NT being a minimum.”
  [p. 9](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf#page=9).
- Molenaar–Verbeek (1♣–1NT, 1♦–2♣) and Helmich–Hop (1♣–1NT) use GF relays
  in place of, not after, a GF minor. Their relay schedules are not on the cards.

## Implications for Muscat

### Evidence

- **Transfer completion:** every write-up of 1♣–2♣ (♦) has opener complete
  by default. Toucan uses 2NT for the strong balanced range and a new suit
  for shortness with a 4-card diamond fit. Goetze uses 2M for a 5-card major,
  or to ask for a stop in the other major, and 2NT for 17–19 balanced.
- **Strength split at once:** Mulder–Kolen (bal 11–13 / singleton 11–13 /
  2NT 17–18), Miszewska–Zawiślak (2♦ “week” / 2M 17+) and WJ 2020 1♣–2♠
  (3NT = 12–14, other bids = strong) show the range on opener's first rebid.
  Polish Club 2010/2020 instead show shape and state that a raise or new suit
  says nothing about strength.
- **Shortness by steps:** Selway–Preddy, Mulder–Kolen, Clow–Peden, Toucan,
  Baxter–Lindop and Cardwell–Verhees all have opener show a singleton early
  after a minor fit.
- **Stops:** WJ 2020 (third suit = four cards or a stop; 2NT = both stops;
  opener's 2NT/3NT split by stop and 4-card length), Goetze (2M = stops),
  Vogt–Fresen and Yadlin–Orenshtein (suit bids show stops after a raise).
- **3NT vs 4m:** 3NT is “17-19 bal” (Fallenius–Welland), “18/19”
  (Cardwell–Verhees), “18-19” (Clow–Peden), but “12-14” in WJ 2020 1♣–2♠.
  Minor keycard is 4♣ (Goetze, Toucan option), 4m in strong sequences
  (Goodman–Short) or 4m+1 Kickback-style (Molenaar–Verbeek, Watson).

### Analysis

- **1♣–2♣ (diamonds):** Muscat's balanced 1♣ has at most 3♦, and 4414 opens
  1♣. A 4-card diamond fit therefore means an unbalanced opener, which is
  exactly Toucan's “new suit = singleton” case. Completing 2♦ can stay the
  default (balanced or no fit), with 2NT for 18–20 balanced as in Toucan
  and Goetze.
- **1♣–2♠ (clubs):** with no fit question (opener has 2+♣ and responder
  5+♣), the WJ 2020 1♣–2♠ model (3NT = weak balanced, other bids = strong or
  shapely) and the Mulder–Kolen split (balanced min / singleton / strong
  balanced) are the closest published layouts.
- **Notrump right-siding:** WJ 2020 and Toucan have the hand with the stop
  bid notrump. This matches the right-siding note in polish.club's
  `NOTES.md`. It is a design choice, not a quoted agreement for these exact
  auctions.
- **Keycard:** a direct 4♣ is free after 1♣–2♠ (clubs agreed), as in Goetze. After
  1♣–2♣ with diamonds agreed, 4♦ would sit at the same level. No checked
  source defines minor keycard specifically after a GF minor response, so
  any choice (4m, Kickback 4m+1, Redwood) has general support but no direct
  precedent.
