# Direct minor-suit responses to a short 1♣

Research checked 9 October 2026. This note tests the planned
**1♣–2♣ = diamonds** and **1♣–2♠ = clubs** against published agreements
for T-Walsh and comparable short or natural 1♣ openings. It concerns
responder's first bid, unpassed and uncontested, unless a source says
otherwise. It records evidence only and does not change Muscat's system.

Ranges are quoted as printed. “Points” and unlabelled ranges are not
converted to HCP. When a card names a strength category with no number,
the table says “no range”. Most entries come from convention cards, which
define the response but rarely the follow-ups.

Sweep coverage: every T-Walsh-like card among the 577 Transnational Swiss
Cup cards on r.pzbs.pl from 2021–2024, 58 cards linked from NBB pages
(Meesterklasse, Topcircuit and Lombard events from 2022 to 2026), and the
authored write-ups already used in this folder.

## 1♣–2♣ as a diamond transfer, or another diamond route

**Where a direct 2♣ (or natural 2♦) shows diamonds after a short 1♣, it is
usually game-forcing with 5+♦. Two authored write-ups add a weak branch.
None of the checked sources uses an INV+ diamond transfer.**

## Opener's continuations that are documented

- **Toucan 1♣–2♣:** opener completes with two exceptions. 2NT shows
  “the 18-19 HCP balanced hand”. With four diamonds and an unbalanced hand,
  a new suit shows a singleton, or a void with a minimum. A jump shows a
  void and well above a minimum. [PDF p. 12](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf#page=12).
- **Toucan 1♣–2♠:** if willing to play 3NT opposite the weak hand,
  opener rebids 2NT, and a too-weak responder signs off in 3♣. If
  responder bids on, it shows the SJS hand, naming a shortness (a jump
  shows a void), or 3NT with no shortness.
  [PDF pp. 12–13](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf#page=12).
- **Goetze 1♣–2♣:** opener bids 2♦ “if willing to play there opposite the
  weak variant”. Noncompletion is forcing. A two-level major has 5 cards
  or a weak holding in the other major. 2NT shows 17–19 balanced. Bidding
  on after completion is natural and game-forcing.
  [p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=10).
- **Watson 1♣–2♣ (GF):** opener accepts “unless holding an unexpected hand”.
  [p. 8](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf#page=8).
- **Inverted raises after a 2+ club:** Mulder–Kolen answer 1♣–2♣ with
  “2♦=bal 11-13 2♥/2♠=si 11-13 2Sa=17-18 3+♣”
  ([p. 2](https://r.pzbs.pl/2024/tsc/web/CC/CC%20NLD%202%20Mulder-Kolen.pdf#page=2)).
  Selway–Preddy use steps: “1st step = major singleton, 2nd step = minor
  singleton, 3rd/4th step = BAL, Other = Void”
  ([p. 2](https://r.pzbs.pl/2022/tsc/web/CC/CC%20ENG%20Hackett%20Selway-Preddy.pdf#page=2)).
  For Fallenius–Welland, opener's 2NT or 3m is non-forcing, anything else
  creates a GF, and 3NT shows 17–19 balanced
  ([p. 5](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf#page=5)).
  In Bridge World Standard, three of the minor shows willingness to play
  there, and 2NT is natural but forced to 3m. Goded–Goded: “1m-2m 2m+1
  strong, asks for short”.
- **No continuations were found** for Molenaar–Verbeek's 2♣/2♠, Clow–Peden,
  Watson's 2♠, or the constructive 6–9 2♠ cards.

## Implications for Muscat

### Evidence

- **2♣ = ♦:** the direct short-club precedents are GF (Watson, Molenaar–Verbeek
  in every version, Clow–Peden, Gipson). Natural analogues are also GF:
  2♦ in Fallenius–Welland and Walsh–Goodman, and 2♣/2♦ in Polish Club.
  Toucan and Goetze instead make 2♣ **weak-or-GF**. No source uses an INV+
  diamond transfer. Invitational diamond hands have their own bid:
  Goetze 2♦ (10–12, 6+♦), Fallenius–Welland 3♦ (about 10–11, 6+),
  Polish Club 3♦ (9–11, six), Van den Bos–Lucassen 2♠ (inv, 6+♦) and
  probably Molenaar–Verbeek 2026 2♠.
- **Diamond length:** every GF version that states a length allows 5♦
  (Molenaar–Verbeek and Clow–Peden give none), and Watson, Goetze and
  Polish Club allow a 4-card major. The weak and invitational versions ask
  for 6+♦ (Goetze, Fallenius–Welland, Polish Club).
- **2♠ = ♣:** INV+ is documented in short-club T-Walsh systems
  (Watson, 6+♣; Molenaar–Verbeek 2022, “fit”; Rigal–Milgrim;
  Hargreaves–McOrmond, “LR+”). Limit-only versions are documented too
  (Meckstroth–Zia, Lin–Rosenberg, Weichsel–Krekorian). So are
  **constructive 6–9 5+♣** versions (Czyzowicz–Jacob, Mikkelsen–Furunes,
  Klimowicz–Lebi), usually alongside an INV+ or GF 2♣.
- **Opener's continuations:** the transfer continuations found are
  “complete unless strong balanced or unbalanced” (Toucan, Goetze), with
  2NT showing the strong balanced range. For club raises, opener's first
  rebid splits minimum from strong (Mulder–Kolen, Selway–Preddy,
  Fallenius–Welland, BWS).

### Analysis

- **2♣ = ♦ at INV+** has no direct precedent. The evidence favours either
  **GF, 5+♦** (simple; matches every T-Walsh card found) or a split such
  as Goetze's (weak 6+♦ / GF 5+♦, with invitations elsewhere). If Muscat
  keeps INV+, the transfer completion has to carry the information that
  GF-only systems never need: is opener minimum, and does opener have
  18–20 balanced? Toucan's “2NT = strong balanced, otherwise complete”
  is the nearest model.
- **2♠ = ♣ at INV+** is the better supported of the two plans. It matches
  Watson, Molenaar–Verbeek 2022, Rigal–Milgrim and Hargreaves–McOrmond.
  Most of these leave length unstated. Watson's 6+♣ is the only explicit
  INV+ length found; the 6–9 cards use 5+♣. Opposite a possible doubleton,
  5+♣ is the minimum seen in an artificial 2♠ raise. 4+♣ appears only in
  natural 2♣ raises (Selway–Preddy, Kiljan–Nab, Drijver–Nab, Kokish–Kraft).
- **“Completion = minimum” over 2♠:** Muscat's 1♣ has both 12–14 and
  18–20 balanced. Treating 2♠–2NT (or the first step) as a minimum or
  balanced answer resembles Mulder–Kolen's 2♦ = “bal 11-13” and
  2NT = “17-18 3+♣”. This is a design choice, not a quoted agreement.
- **Weak minor hands are still unassigned:** 0–7 club raises use 2NT or
  3♣ in many cards (Molenaar–Verbeek, Kiljan–Nab, Drijver–Nab, Watson,
  Goetze). Weak diamond hands use 1♠, 1NT or a 3♦ preempt. Muscat's 1♠
  (4+ HCP, no major) already covers the weak club and diamond hands that
  do not want to preempt.
