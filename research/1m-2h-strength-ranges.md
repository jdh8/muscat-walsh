# Direct 1♣/1♦–2♥: a survey of strength ranges

Research checked 8 October 2026. This concerns responder's **first bid in an
uncontested auction**, with responder unpassed unless the source says otherwise.
It surveys agreements beyond T-Walsh and does not change Muscat's system.

**Reverse Flannery has no universal strength range.** The checked sources
include sub-invitational, wide limited and invitational versions of the direct
2♥ response. Some partnerships give it different ranges after 1♣ and 1♦.
Examples include Levin–Weinstein's **9–11 HCP after 1♣ versus 4–8 after 1♦**,
and Lin–Rosenberg's **under 8 HCP after 1♣ versus under 11 after 1♦**.
[Levin–Weinstein, supplementary sheet](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/WeinsteinLevinWBF2022.pdf#page=3),
[Lin–Rosenberg, 2026 Mixed USBC system summary](https://mail.usbf.org/apps/teamforms.php?fid=4144&pid=1310&task=display).

## Reverse Flannery: documented direct 2♥ ranges

The shape is written in **♠–♥ order**. An omitted plus sign is deliberate:
exact 5–4 and at least 5–4 should not be silently combined. Likewise, “points”
and an unlabelled range are retained when the source does not explicitly say
HCP. These are examples of agreements, not evidence of their relative popularity.

## Why the opening minor matters

Levin–Weinstein use **1♣–2♦ for the weak 4–8 HCP major two-suiter**;
this leaves 1♣–2♥ for the stronger 9–11 exact 5–4 hand. Over 1♦,
2♥ takes the weak hands and 2♠ takes 9–11 HCP with 5+♠ and four hearts.
Their club 2♠ separately shows six spades and four hearts, 9–11 HCP.
[Supplementary sheet, note 1](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/WeinsteinLevinWBF2022.pdf#page=3).

Lin–Rosenberg allocate the club jumps in the opposite strength order:
**1♣–2♦ is 8–10 HCP**, while **1♣–2♥ is under 8**, both with
5+♠–4+♥. After 1♦, the single 2♥ route combines hands under 11 HCP.
[2026 system summary](https://mail.usbf.org/apps/teamforms.php?fid=4144&pid=1310&task=display).

Kokish–Kraft explain their broad diamond range explicitly: 2♥ is the only
Reverse Flannery response available for 5–4, 5–5 and 6–5 hands, whereas
clubs have 2♦ for the weak branch. Their club invitation is evaluated as a
hand worth bidding game opposite a normal strong notrump with a decent fit;
opener may still pass 2♥. A good 11+ diamond responder starts 1♠ and
forces to game later. **6+♠–exactly 4♥ invitational** hands have a separate
2♠ response over either minor.
[Notes, printed pp. 9 and 11](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf#page=9).

These examples show why “weak,” “limited” and “invitational” are not
interchangeable numerical categories. In particular, the effective
**7–11 HCP** club range above is explicitly invitational, while the diamond
range deliberately spans weaker hands through invitations.
[Kokish–Kraft, pp. 9 and 11](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf#page=9).

## The same auction can mean something else

A strength survey of 1m–2♥ must identify the hand type first. The following
are **alternative agreements**, not extra ranges for the major two-suiter.

| Meaning of direct 2♥ | Documented strength and shape |
| --- | --- |
| Natural weak jump shift after either minor | Todd gives **usually 0–5 HCP**, **6+♥**, or about **3–6 HCP when vulnerable**, explicitly listing both 1♣–2♥ and 1♦–2♥ among uncontested examples. [Author's February 2024 lesson](https://www.advinbridge.com/this-week-in-bridge/512). |
| Strong jump shift after either minor | Fallenius–Welland's 2002 T-Walsh club table puts 2♥/2♠ at **14+ HCP**, potentially with a side four-card suit. Their diamond table specifies **14+ with a 6+ card suit**, also allowing a side four-card suit. The entries call these jump shifts without explicitly labelling GF. [Partnership notes, printed pp. 3 and 5](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf#page=3). |
| Standard strong jump shift | ACBL's glossary gives **19+ points including distribution** for standard methods, and describes several possible hand types. It separately identifies the strong jump shift by an unpassed hand as forcing. This is a generic jump-shift rule, not a Reverse Flannery agreement. [ACBL glossary, “JUMP SHIFT” and “FORCING BID”](https://www.acbl.org/learn/). |
| Minor two-suiter with heart shortage after T-Walsh 1♣ | Goetze gives **at least 5–4 either way in the minors**, **0–1♥**, and **9–12 or very strong**; this is a split-range artificial response. [Author's manuscript, printed p. 4](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=4). |
| Minor two-suiter after a strong artificial 1♣ | Watson's New Age Precision uses **6–8 HCP**, **5–5 or 6–5 in the minors**. Its 1♣ is 16+ HCP unbalanced or 17+ balanced; Reverse Flannery is instead **1♣–2♦**, exactly 5♠–4♥ and 6+ HCP, forcing. [Author's notes, p. 1](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/NAP_b2.pdf#page=1). |

No checked source in this note establishes **direct 1m–2♥, 5+♠–4+♥,
GF** as an additional Reverse Flannery range. GF references to
**1m–1♠; 2m–2♥** concern responder's later rebid and must not be used
as evidence for the direct response. The survey's positive evidence supports
the weak, broad limited and invitational categories above; it does not
establish that other agreements are impossible.

## Reading the ranges when designing Muscat — analysis

There are three distinct choices supported by the precedents:

- **A weak bucket plus a separate invitation:** choose the lower threshold
  deliberately, and reserve another route for invitational major two-suiters.
  Cohen, Todd, BWS and Watson supply examples of this allocation.
- **A single broad limited bucket:** state how opener distinguishes a very
  weak responder from an invitation before committing to game. The broad
  Kokish–Kraft diamond range is an explicit precedent, while Lin–Rosenberg's
  diamond summary provides a modern under-11 example.
- **An invitational direct 2♥:** provide a separate weak route, such as a
  direct 2♦ over clubs or the ordinary one-level response. The
  Levin–Weinstein club treatment and Sampson's one-way version illustrate
  this choice.

These are deductions from the sourced allocations, not proposed new
agreements. Any adoption also needs the exact allowed shapes, passability,
opener's enquiries and signoffs, the GF route, and interference rules.
The name “Reverse Flannery” alone cannot supply those details.
