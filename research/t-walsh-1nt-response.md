# Direct 1♣–1NT responses with T-Walsh

Research checked 8 October 2026. This concerns responder's **first** bid,
not opener's 1NT rebid after a major transfer. The sources document several
different agreements; they do not establish which is most popular.

The follow-up investigation of **club transfers and GF relays** is in this
same note, below the survey. Source agreements and proposed Muscat adaptations
are distinguished throughout.

The **both-major response**, including Lia's current exact-4–4 description
and the distinction from a 4+–4+ treatment, is examined in
[a separate follow-up](t-walsh-both-majors.md).

The Fallenius–Welland invitation is natural and limited; “invitational” should
not be read as forcing to game. Their 11–13 response range belongs to their
11–13 minimum balanced opening range, rather than being a universal T-Walsh
range. [Partnership notes, pp. 3–4](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf#page=3).

Goetze gives another natural, limited variant: **7–11 balanced with five
diamonds or 3=3=4=3**. His 1♠ instead promises 4+ clubs and 7+ points,
denying a four-card major. After 1NT, opener's 2♣/2♦ are to play, 2♥/2♠
are reverses, and 2NT/3♣ invite. Thus this is not simply a generic weak NT
response copied into a no-major 1♠ structure. [Authored manuscript,
printed pp. 4, 10–11](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=4).

## Versions and limits of the evidence

Gipson explicitly reported changing to **1NT GF relay** in September 2012,
with 1♠ now 5–12 without a major and 2♣ game forcing with five diamonds.
He explains that they adapted a relay method already used after 1M–2♣.
The 2008 natural agreement and 2012 relay agreement are different versions,
not contradictory descriptions of one system. [Firsthand account,
posts #4 and #12](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/55506-transfer-responses-to-a-natural-short-club/page__p__666240).

A separate firsthand diamond method uses **1NT as a transfer to diamonds,
either strong or very weak**, with 1♠ transferring to notrump. That account
does not provide complete length/range requirements; it should not be merged
with ahydra's limited 5–9 agreement. [“helene_t,” September 2005,
post #4](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/10175-transfer-walsh-responses/).

## Club transfer: what the published method actually provides

Beall's Toucan article supports these continuations. The initial transfer
forces a bid but permits a subsequent partscore signoff. **Forcing does not
establish that 2♣ is compulsory on every opener's hand.**

| Opener after 1♣–1NT | What the checked public sources establish |
| --- | --- |
| Pass | Excluded: the response is forcing. |
| 2♣ | Documented transfer completion; no separate strength/fit condition is given. |
| 2♦ / 2♥ / 2♠ / 2NT / higher | No verified assignment for this auction. |

The following table describes responder's actions **when opener completes
to 2♣**, rather than a rule that opener always completes.

| Auction | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| 1♣–1NT; 2♣–Pass | Weak clubs, stopping in 2♣. |
| 1♣–1NT; 2♣–3NT | Responder chooses to declare notrump, typically protecting tenaces. |
| 1♣–1NT; 2♣–2♥ | GF with 5+ clubs and 5+ hearts: minor first, then major. |

[Beall's authored explanation](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/esoterica/toucan.html).

The free 2020 manuscript puts **club invitations through 1♠**, then 2♣
over opener's 1NT. It does not give a complete opener-rebid table after
the direct club transfer, or a numerical weak-hand range. Its explicit
18–19 balanced exception over **1♣–2♣**, a diamond transfer, does not
establish an identical exception over **1♣–1NT**. These gaps matter when
adapting the method to Muscat's 18–20 hands. [Manuscript,
PDF pp. 11–13 and 15](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf#page=11).

There is also a narrower club-transfer treatment: Watson's base system uses
1NT for **GF, 5+ clubs plus another four-card suit**. This differs materially
from Toucan's weak-inclusive use. [Watson, 12 December 2025,
pp. 3 and 8](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf#page=3).

### Fit with Muscat — analysis

The benefit is an extra step below a direct 2♣ response, an immediate club
description, and separation of weak and invitational club routes. Opener
has already bid clubs, so the transfer's distinctive benefit here is bidding
space; opener would declare a club contract after a natural raise too.

There are three decisions before this becomes a complete agreement:

- **Weak club length:** five clubs opposite a two-card opening can leave a
  seven-card fit. Decide which weak five-card club hands prefer the no-major
  1♠ route, retaining the chance to play 1NT, and which insist on clubs.
- **Strong opener:** define when 18–20 balanced or a strong unbalanced opener
  completes to 2♣ and when opener breaks the transfer. Always completing and
  allowing a weak responder to pass could miss game; always jumping can
  carry a very weak responder too high.
- **Invitations and other minors:** adopting Toucan's invitational
  1♣–1♠; 1NT–2♣ means that auction cannot simultaneously be a weak club
  signoff. Define responder's GF club routes and the direct 2♣/2♦ responses
  together. The club transfer alone does not force adoption of every Toucan
  response.

## GF relay: precedent versus a usable continuation structure

Molenaar–Verbeek is the close partnership precedent: its card confirms
1NT GF relay, no-major 1♠, and 18–20 for opener's 1NT after a major transfer.
However, it gives no direct 1♣–1NT answer table or responder shape eligibility.
The card's answers after **1M–2♣** describe a different auction. I did not
locate a public full relay manuscript for this pair or for Gipson's later
method. [Molenaar–Verbeek card](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf),
[Gipson's firsthand report](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/55506-transfer-responses-to-a-natural-short-club/page__p__666240).

Watson supplies optional **GF shape relays**, pp. 27–37. Responder normally
asks again with the next denomination; responder's 3NT is to play.
One concrete branch is:

| Auction | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1♣–1NT; 2NT | Opener has a 4-3-3-3 shape. |
| …3♣ | Responder asks which four-card suit. |
| …3♦ / 3♥ / 3♠ / 3NT | Opener shows 3=3=3=4 / 3=3=4=3 / 3=4=3=3 / 4=3=3=3, in ♠♥♦♣ order. |

His relay version reallocates GF clubs plus another four-card suit to 1♠,
conflicting with Muscat's no-major guarantee. The opening scheme differs,
responder eligibility is incompletely specified, and the PDF ends with shape
tables rather than the promised later strength/control methods.
[Watson's optional appendix, especially pp. 29 and 32–33](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf#page=32).

### Fit with Muscat — analysis

A GF relay makes responder the questioner and opener the hand being described.
It is attractive when responder benefits more from discovering opener's shape
than from immediately describing their own. Responder also becomes declarer
in an eventual notrump contract, because responder first bid notrump.

Muscat would need to define:

- **Eligible hands:** all game forces, selected balanced hands, or another
  subset; whether four-card majors are allowed. “GF relay” alone settles none
  of these choices.
- **Opener's answers:** cover every permitted 1♣ shape and distinguish
  12–14 from 18–20, as well as the unbalanced strength ranges. A shape-only
  branch is insufficient for deciding between game and slam.
- **After the shape answers:** when bidding becomes natural, how to agree
  trumps, and how to investigate controls and keycards; also what survives
  interference.
- **Hands excluded from the relay:** preserve explicit routes for weak and
  invitational clubs and for GF club/major hands while keeping 1♠ strictly
  no-major if that remains Muscat's preference.

## Comparison for Muscat — analysis

Muscat currently assigns 1♠ to 4+ HCP without a four-card major, alongside
major-showing 1♦/1♥ responses. [Current response table](../src/1C.md).
Its balanced ladder is 12–14 / 15–17 / 18–20.
[Current opening notes](../src/Openings.md).

| Design question | Weak-inclusive club transfer | GF relay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main job | Describe responder's clubs and organize club strength ranges | Discover opener's shape, then strength and controls |
| Can this response lead to 2♣ as the final contract? | Yes | No |
| What does opener initially learn? | Usually club length, potentially weak | Game-going strength, with shape depending on the agreed eligibility |
| Who declares eventual notrump? | Responder | Responder |
| Most important Muscat adaptation | Strong-opener actions and the weak/invitational club split | Complete answers for Muscat's shapes and strength ranges |

**The choice depends on which hands need help:** the club transfer organizes
club hands, while a limited both-major response describes weak major hands
immediately. Lia's documented exact-4–4 option is narrower than a 4+–4+
treatment; the latter needs additional fit-finding agreements. See the
[both-major follow-up](t-walsh-both-majors.md). The GF relay remains a larger
constructive-bidding project. None of the checked sources yet supplies a
complete continuation structure adapted to Muscat for these three options.

This note records research and design implications; the system definitions
in `src/1C.md` have not been changed.
