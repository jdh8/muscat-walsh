# Continuations after a no-major 1♣–1♠

Research checked 9 October 2026. This note collects published continuations
after **1♣–1♠ denying a four-card major**. It records evidence only; Muscat's
choices are in `src/1C/1S.md`. Ranges are quoted as printed. The Bridge World
Toucan article was read from the archive.org copy of 14 July 2024.

## Sources

**Ray Green, revised February 2016.** 1NT opens 15–16. 1♠ is "a relay
denying a 4 card major. Responder may be balanced, or have a long minor";
strength not stated. Opener "rebids clubs with 6, else bids 1NT 12-14, 2NT
17/18, and with 19/20 bids 2♥ to puppet 2♠ then rebids 2NT." 3♣ over the
puppet shows a heart reverse, the only way opener shows a major. After 1NT,
responder may "pass or raise if balanced (eg 2NT on 11/12), bid 2♣ or 2♦ to
play, bid 3♣ or 3♦ as GF, or bid 2M … as a game invitation in the
corresponding minor." [Article](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html);
[PDF copy, p. 3](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Transfer-Walsh.pdf#page=3).

**Bruce Watson, December 2025.** 1♣ also includes any strong two-bid. 1♠ is
"No 4-card major and possibly weak. Catchall"; balanced 11–12 and 13–15 bid
2NT and 3NT directly. Opener: 1NT is "Some minimum opener. Often the weak NT.
XYZ."; 2♣ is the strong two-bid; 2♦/2M are "Standard reverse with longer
clubs"; 2NT is "18-19 HCP"; 3♦/3M are autosplinters; 3NT is running clubs.
After 1NT, responder uses extended XYZ with two-under transfers.
[PDF, pp. 3, 7–8, 17–18](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf#page=7).

**Molenaar–Verbeek.** The card says "1♣-1♠ = No Major". The IMP article
adds that 1♠ is a catchall for weak and invitational hands without a major,
plus game forces with both minors or wanting opener to declare notrump. Other
game forces start with a 1NT relay. Opener rebids notrump, "(1SA 12-14; 2SA
18-19)", or a suit to show an unbalanced hand. Responder's continuations are
not stated.
[NBB card, PDF pp. 2–3](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf#page=2);
[IMP, p. 30](https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/v3/95b379df2f94d1433ff82969af2dfd35c83e1535.pdf#page=30).

**Toucan, Beall 2020.** 1♠ "suggests opener rebid 1NT… usually shows a hand
with no four card major and minimum modest values", but may also hold
balanced or long-minor game hands, slammish hands, or game-forcing 5–5
majors. Opener rebids 1NT with a normal minimum and otherwise bids naturally.
After 1NT, 2♣ is "natural, invitational"; other suit bids are slammish.
[Free PDF, pp. 8–9, 13](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf#page=8).

**Fallenius–Welland, 2002.** 1NT opens 14–16. 1♠ is a "Transfer to 1NT"
covering 6+ balanced or semi-balanced hands, weak club raises, weak long
diamonds, and some game forces with diamonds and a major. Balanced invites and
game forces respond 1NT and 2NT directly. Opener bids "1NT with 11-13 bal,
but 2♦ with 17-19 bal"; reverses are the only way to show a major. Over 1NT,
"Minor suit bids are NF"; a major is a game force with diamonds.
[Notes, pp. 3–4](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf#page=3).

Cards with the same response but no continuations:

## Patterns

1. A minimum balanced opener rebids 1NT, 12–14 or 11–13, and never shows a
   major.
2. Strong balanced hands differ: 2NT 18–19 (Watson, IMP), 2NT 17–18 with a
   puppet for 19–20 (Green), or 2♦ 17–19 (Fallenius–Welland).
3. Opener shows a major only by a reverse with longer clubs.
4. After 1NT, two-level minors are to play or non-forcing. Invitations and
   game forces are artificial, and the methods differ.
5. No source states how a balanced game-forcing responder continues after
   1♣–1♠–1NT. Watson and Fallenius–Welland avoid the question by responding
   in notrump directly.
