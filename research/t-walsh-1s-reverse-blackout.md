# Blackout after a Transfer Walsh 1♣–1♠ reverse

Checked 13 October 2026. The relevant 1♠ response is artificial, not a
natural spade suit. This note distinguishes published agreements from
auction-specific deductions; Muscat's current treatment is in
[`src/1C/1S.md`](../src/1C/1S.md).

**The closest named precedent found is Mundell–Peake's “Blackout (next
step)”.** Their card also documents transfer responses and an artificial
1♠. However, it does not print the three reverse auctions or opener's
continuations. Two other partnerships combine Transfer Walsh and Blackout
on their cards, with different or unstated negative bids. No inspected
source supplies Muscat's complete 4–7 / 16–17 / 18–20 structure.

## A manuscript with a general Blackout-like rule: Umeno–Sher

[Marc Umeno–Josh Sher, *Supernatural*, §§6.3.4 and 6.12](https://bridgewithdan.com/supernatural26april03/)
labels 2♦/2♥/2♠ after 1♣–1♠ as reverses. Its artificial 1♠ followups
include long-diamond hands; this is not a natural spade response.
The general reverse section allows weakness through the cheaper fourth suit
or 2NT, permits passing opener's next minimum bid, and makes other bids
natural and GF subject to its own-major exception. Reverses normally show
17+ or slightly less with extra shape.

This supports a **Blackout-like general mechanism**, but the manuscript does
not call it Blackout or explicitly resolve “fourth suit” after artificial
1♠. It does not publish the three negative calls or a relay-response table.
Do not claim an exact no-major, cheapest-step agreement from it.

## An exact auction with a weak relay: Aggarwal–Rele

[Sudhir Aggarwal, “Transfer Responses over 1C (Revisited)”, Delhi Bridge
Association, April 2006, PDF p. 1](https://www.bridgewebs.com/dba/Transfer%20Responses%20over%201C.pdf#page=1)
describes methods played with Amod Rele. 1♠ denies four-card majors and
includes balanced 5–11 hands and minor-suit hands. The following rows are
explicit:

| Auction | Published continuation |
| --- | --- |
| 1♣–1♠; 2♦ | 17–19 balanced **or** a light diamond reverse |
| 1♣–1♠; 2♦–2♥ | Sign-off or relay |
| …2♥–2♠ | Diamond reverse |
| …2♥–2NT | Balanced 17–19 |
| …2♥–3♣ | Six clubs and 4+ diamonds |
| 1♣–1♠; 2♦–2NT | Natural forcing, 10–11 |
| 1♣–1♠; 2♦–3♣/3♦ | Natural forcing |

This is an exact 2♥ weak-relay precedent, but **not a source calling it
Blackout**. Its 2♦ is two-way and its relay need not show weakness. The
article lists 2♥/2♠ as other reverses without responder continuation
tables. Its 2♠ relay answer contrasts with Muscat, which allows 2NT as a
minimum answer immediately after the negative.

## Counterexamples that prevent a universal claim

- [Ash–Bayer, §§3.1–3.1.1, printed p. 9](https://www.dubiouslogic.com/bridge/system/macc/docs/MA_AB-Jan-2017%202.pdf#page=9)
  explicitly excludes 1♣–1♠ from Blackout because responder has not shown
  a suit. The same section lists all three opener reverses as F1 and
  promises another bid. Thus their Blackout after a red transfer cannot be
  transplanted to the no-major response as a claimed partnership agreement.
- [Michael Goetze, *2/1 T-Walsh*, §§2.1.7–2.2, pp. 8–9](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=8)
  uses the cheaper fourth suit / 2NT mechanism under the name Ingberman
  after the **red transfers**. His 1♠ instead promises 4+ clubs and 7+
  points, no four-card major. Opener's 2♦/2♥/2♠ show 16+, club support and
  values in the bid suit; these reverses force only to 3♣. Responder shows
  values at the two level and shortage at the three level. This separate
  section does not prescribe Blackout.

## What happens after the negative bid?

Further searches found no public fuller reverse notes for Mundell–Peake,
Gosney–Thomson or Beckett–Ewart. The [current Bridge NSW listing](https://bridgensw.com.au/systems/)
marks Mundell–Peake revised 19 December 2024, but its linked two-page card
adds no relay continuation. The following **red-transfer comparisons** are
documented; they do not establish these partnerships' no-major auctions.

**Ash–Bayer, §§3.1.2–3.1.3, pp. 9–10:** after
1♣–1♥; 2♦–2♥, responder showed spades then bid Blackout. Opener can
describe shape instead of completing a compulsory relay:

| Opener's next bid | Treatment; responder's subsequent weak options |
| --- | --- |
| 2♠ | Typically 1345, NF; 2NT/3♣/3♦ are NF |
| 2NT | Minimum, typically 1345, NF; 3♣/3♦ are NF |
| 3♣ | NF; responder's 3♦ is NF |
| 3♦ | F1, 5/6+ diamonds; responder's 4♣/4♦ are passable |
| 3♥/3♠ | GF |

Shapes are spades–hearts–diamonds–clubs. For the **actual 1♣–1♠ reverse**,
§3.1.3 instead makes 2NT and 3♣ natural NF; new suits are GF features;
1♣–1♠; 2♦–3♦ is natural NF.
[Authored notes, p. 10](https://www.dubiouslogic.com/bridge/system/macc/docs/MA_AB-Jan-2017%202.pdf#page=10).

**Goetze, §2.1.7:** opener accepts a possible sign-off by bidding the step;
responder then chooses the final contract. Applying that stated rule to the
listed red-transfer reverses gives:

| Auction through responder's negative | Opener's accepting step |
| --- | --- |
| 1♣–1♦; 2♦–2♠ | 2NT |
| 1♣–1♥; 2♦–2♥ | 2♠ |
| 1♣–1♥; 2♥–2NT | 3♣ |
| 1♣–1♦; 2♠–2NT | 3♣ |

These are deductions from his step rule, not printed examples.
He does not tabulate stronger opener replies. This mechanism differs from
Ash–Bayer's multiple descriptive minimum bids.
[Authored notes, pp. 8–9](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=8).

## General Blackout descriptions: natural rebids

These accounts concern **natural responses**, not the no-major 1♠.
[David Loeb](https://loebbridge.com/index.php/bridge-articles/blackout-over-openers-reverse)
typically repeats opener's first suit with a minimum after Blackout;
responder may pass. With extras, opener avoids that cheapest first-suit
rebid and shows strength and shape.

[Dee Harley, VBA October 2021, pp. 3–4](https://bridgevictoria.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/vbabulletin2110.pdf#page=3)
gives concrete replies after **1♦–1♠; 2♥–2NT**:

| Opener's holding | Reply and responder's choice |
| --- | --- |
| Six diamonds, four hearts, minimum | 3♦; responder passes |
| 1453, minimum | 3♣ on three clubs; responder passes or prefers 3♦/3♥ |
| Strong six-diamond/four-heart hand | 3NT |
| Strong six-diamond/five-heart hand | 4♥; responder passes or corrects to 5♦ |

Thus Blackout need not entail a compulsory accepting step. These examples
illustrate the mechanism, not Muscat's strength bands or club-opening replies.

## What this establishes for Muscat

A cheapest-step negative after an artificial 1♠ has a close card-level
precedent and an exact 2♦–2♥ weak-relay analogue. The sources do **not**
establish a Transfer Walsh consensus, a compulsory 3♣ relay, or Muscat's
specific minimum-opener shape and strength answers. Those remain partnership
design choices. A no-major response also removes the natural own-major
rebid exception found in standard Blackout descriptions; tables written for
a natural 1♠ response are not directly applicable.
