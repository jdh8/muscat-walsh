# Transfer Walsh with a 1♦ opening containing four diamonds and five clubs

Research checked 13 October 2026. The question concerns Muscat's
`src/Openings.md`: 1♣–1♦/1♥ shows hearts/spades, and 11–17 hands with
four or more diamonds and exactly five clubs open 1♦ unless suitable for
1NT. Shape notation below is spades–hearts–diamonds–clubs.

## Confirmed published partnership precedents

**Yes: published convention cards explicitly combine Transfer Walsh and a
1♦ opening containing 4♦5♣. Mike–Sarah Bell supply the clearest stated
strength range, 11–15.** These are partnership systems documented in public
cards, rather than complete system books. None of the cards checked explicitly
states Muscat's full 11–17 rule, including every `(xx)45` shape.

| Partnership and primary source | Evidence for 4♦5♣ opening 1♦ | Evidence for Transfer Walsh | Differences from Muscat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Mike–Sarah Bell, EBU card](https://www.ebu.co.uk/documents/competitions/convention-cards/Bell-Bell.pdf) | Page 1 system summary and page 2 1♦ row explicitly include **4♦5♣, 11–15** in an unbalanced diamond opening. | Page 1 special bids and page 2 1♣ responses explicitly give 1♦=4+♥ and 1♥=4+♠. | 11–15 rather than 11–17; 1♦ normally unbalanced, so this does not document the weak 2245 case. 1NT is 15–17 except 14–16 first seat nonvulnerable. |

Always unbalanced 1♦ does not document 2245. |

The Cope–Cruise card's Note 12(b) renders the first opening as `2D` in web
text extraction while describing a 4+♦/5+♣ subsequent auction. That apparent
internal typo is unnecessary to the conclusion: the 1♦ opening row is explicit.

## A narrower authored-system precedent

[Bruce Watson, *Standard with Transfer Responses to 1♣*, 12 December 2025](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf)
explicitly opens 1♦ with **0=4=4=5 and 11–14 HCP** (printed pp. 2–3 and 9).
Printed p. 3 gives 1♣–1♦=4+♥ and 1♣–1♥=4+♠. This confirms the idea for one
particular `(xx)45` shape, but does not transfer all such shapes to 1♦.
The 1♣ opening otherwise includes 11–19 with five clubs and another four-card
suit, and any strong two-bid. Thus it is a narrower shape exception in a
materially different opening structure, not Muscat's exact rule.

## Existing references which do not prove this treatment

- [Molenaar–Verbeek, NBB MK2526 card](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf):
  the actual downloaded PDF's 1♦ opening row allows four diamonds only with
  4441 and a singleton club. Therefore it **does not** document 4♦5♣ in 1♦,
  despite its closely matching Transfer Walsh strength ladder.
- [Michael Goetze, *2/1 T-Walsh*, printed p. 11](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf):
  four-card diamonds are restricted to exactly 4441 or 1444. Therefore not a
  precedent for `(xx)45`.
- [Ray Green's authored Transfer Walsh article](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html):
  the opening-selection discussion transfers unbalanced 4♦5♣
  into 1♦, but keeping 2245 outside the notrump range in 1♣. That is a relevant
  partial match, not evidence for Muscat's whole `(xx)45` rule.

## Public discussion, weaker than a formal system publication

A firsthand [BBO post by Adebisi, 2012, #18](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/55506-transfer-responses-to-a-natural-short-club/page__p__666240)
recommends 1♦ with 4♦5♣ and **11–15**
when playing Transfer Walsh, and describes a 1♦–1M–2♣ auction containing both
minor orientations. This is useful corroboration that the treatment was already
being discussed in 2012, but the three formal partnership cards above are
stronger evidence of a published played system.

## Limits of the conclusion

The finding establishes a published precedent for **the minor-suit lengths and
the transfer responses**, especially the Bell 11–15 agreement. It does not
establish that another published system uses exactly Muscat's 11–17 boundary,
handles 2245 the same way, or includes every 4+♦/5=♣ hand by the same rule.
The SABF search index reports a recent crawl/publication date, but the
Donde–Stephens card itself explicitly identifies the **2017 trials**; the date
in the document is the relevant system provenance.

No system documents were changed. This research inspected public cards and
free notes; it did not inspect paid system books or claim an exhaustive survey.
