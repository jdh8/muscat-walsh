# One-level free bids and the French “Cachalot”

Checked 15 October 2026. Two questions: what strength standard teaching
assigns to responder's new suit at the one level after an overcall, and what
the French Cachalot (Transfer Walsh in competition) sources actually print.
Quotations are from the authors' own texts unless marked otherwise.

**Conclusion 1.** The mainstream American figure for a one-level free bid is
the uncontested 6+ points; the ACBL booklet says bids keep their uncontested
meaning. The two-level free bid is 10–11+ (ACBL: 11+, five cards). The
documented dissent is historical (Culbertson, 9) or local (ibridge, 8H;
Polish WJ, 7). Suit length is four, except where a negative double would
have shown four (then five).

**Conclusion 2.** Cachalot is the transfer response scheme after a 1♦ or 1♥
overcall of a minor opening: double = the next suit up, 1♥ = spades, 1♠ =
no four-card major (the old Spoutnik double). The 2011 article is by
Olivier Bessis; the 2017 piece is a Soulet–Voldoire “Face à Face” and was
not found online. The transfers start at 6H in Bridge Academy's version;
opener's one-level completion shows exactly three cards.

**Mainstream figure.** Every current American teaching source inspected
keeps the one-level free bid at the uncontested 6+ and treats it as forcing;
the explicit extra-strength requirement is the two-level bid (10–11+, five
cards in ACBL and Cohen). Culbertson's 9 is the historical “free bid shows
extra values” doctrine. The SEF preamble still says a free bid “traduit donc
un supplément de force ou une revalorisation distributionnelle”, but its own
table then prints 6 HL at the one level; ibridge's 8H is a beginner fiche and
the Polish DUCE's 7 is one author's system. No source puts the one-level
free bid at 8+ as a general standard.

**Negative free bids** are a separate agreement that makes the *two-level*
non-jump new suit non-forcing (Walker: 2♣–3♦, 5–10 points, 6+ cards or a
strong five). Walker states one-level and three-level new suits are not
NFBs, so the one-level figure is unaffected.

**Four or five cards.** ACBL, Walker and Cohen give four. Two carve-outs:
Cohen and Walker make 1♠ over a 1♦ overcall five cards because the negative
double shows exactly four spades; Grant (Lindop, July/August 2008) says a
suit bid over an overcall “tends to show a five-card or longer suit” but “A
response of 1♠ after an overcall doesn't guarantee five or more spades”
([betterbridge.com](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200807.pdf)).
Max Hardy's convention-card questionnaire makes the same split a partnership
choice: “Walsh negative doubles … A negative double in this auction denies
four spades, and responder will bid one spade with four or more cards”
([bridge-tips.co.il PDF](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/2_1-Hardy-Max.pdf)).

**Not found.** The *Official Encyclopedia of Bridge* “Free bid” entry, BWS
2017 (paywalled), Hardy's own point figure, and anything from Kantar, Root,
Klinger or Lawrence stating a one-level free-bid minimum. The Bridge Guys
glossary and the BBO SAYC document could not be fetched. Treat the Cohen
example “6+ points (as if there had been no overcall)” quoted in search
summaries as unverified.

## Question 2: Cachalot

**Bibliography (Le Bridgeur index, Union Sportive de Fontenay).** “Le
Cachalot, ou ‘Spoutnik rotatif’”, **Olivier** Bessis, no. 852, July 2011,
p. 56; “Face à Face : Collante ou Cachalot ?”, Philippe Soulet and
Jean-Michel Voldoire, no. 909, May–June 2017, p. 38; Cédric Lorenzini, no.
868, January 2013, p. 46; Alain Lévy, nos. 916–917, 2018
([index](http://usf.bridge.free.fr/bridge/art/bridgeur.html)). Michel Bessis
is Bridge Academy's pedagogical head, whose 2015 course package covers the
convention ([bridge-academy.com](https://www.bridge-academy.com/index.php?r=cours%2Fpackage&id=362));
the original article is Olivier's. The Bessis article text below is from a
[club transcription](http://ghbridge.free.fr/articles_big/E4ZG2.htm), not
a scan. The Soulet–Voldoire piece is cited only by the Chailley course
([PDF](http://www.bridge-chailley.fr/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/E4048-Cachalot.pdf),
giving p. 40); its content was not found.

**Which responses are transfers.** All sources agree on three sequences only:

| Auction | Double | 1♥ | 1♠ | 1NT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1♣ (1♦) | 4+♥ | 4+♠ | no 4-card major; “Texas pour les Sans-Atout” | good stopper |
| 1♣/1♦ (1♥) | 4+♠ | — | denies 4♠; “Correspond à l'ancienne enchère de Contre” | good stopper |

Bessis: “X : Texas, au moins quatre Piques. 1♠ : Dénie quatre Piques … Texas
pour les Sans-Atout. 1SA : Bonne tenue (sinon, on dit 1♠).” Two-level bids
stay natural; Martel: “Les barrages restent inchangés” and with a strong hand
and a long minor “on nomme la longue d'abord”. Bessis offers an optional
2♥ = Texas spades (weak or strong) and 2♠ as the cue-bid. No source applies
Cachalot after a takeout double; Chailley: “Trois séquences concernées
seulement.” Nothing was found for Transfer Walsh-style responses over a
double.

**Minimum strength.** Bessis gives none beyond identifying 1♠ with the old
Spoutnik double (“au moins 8 points d'honneurs”). Claire Martel's note of
the Bridge Academy version tabulates: X* and 1♥* “6H+ illimitée et F1”, 1♠*
“8H+ illimitée et F1”, 1NT 8–10H; and “Les enchères du répondant après
l'intervention restent des enchères libres à partir de 6-7H”
([clairebridge.com PDF](https://www.clairebridge.com/doc_pedagogiques/Jouez_le_cachalot.pdf)).
Lévy 2018: 1♠ is a “Contre d'appel, 8H minimum sans limite supérieure”
([clairebridge.com PDF](https://www.clairebridge.com/doc_pedagogiques/cachalot_alevy.pdf)).
Hegerm's secondary summary gives 8+ HCP for every call
([hegerm.ch](https://www.hegerm.ch/textes/21e_Se.html)); it conflicts with
Martel and is not primary.

**How opener accepts.** The consistent rule is that a one-level completion
shows three cards, never four:

| Source | Completion at 1-level | With four cards | 1NT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bessis 2011 | “L'ouvreur peut dire 1♠ avec trois cartes”; whether it is forcing is left as a question | raise as if responder had bid the suit | “ne dénie normalement pas trois cartes à Pique” |
| Chailley course (Bessis + Voldoire) | “Elle promet 3 cartes exactement dans la couleur” | “il soutient au niveau de 2 ou plus” | preferred with a flat minimum even holding three |
| Martel / Bridge Academy | “promet exactement 3 cartes … jamais 4. Cette enchère est illimitée et forcing un tour” | support from the two level as in standard | may hide three cards; then shows a good stopper |
| Lévy 2018, Rule 1 | rectify only when NT would not have been bid: 4♠, 18–19 balanced without stopper, or any unbalanced 12–22; 6-3-2-2 rectifies with three | — | 2♣ relay, majors by transfer, jumps GF |
| ibridge fiche 64CA-4 | “montre 3 cartes … l'absence d'arrêt” | simple jump = 1st zone, double jump = 2nd zone | “régulière de 1ère zone, l'arrêt” ([ibridge.fr](https://www.ibridge.fr/public/fichepdf/390)) |
| Fauché–Mazères 2011 | three cards, “Non Forcing”, opener having denied 18H | as standard | does not deny three cards nor promise a stopper ([dsbridge.fr PDF](http://dsbridge.fr/documents/technique/Convention%20Cachalot.pdf)) |

Lévy's Rule 1 differs from the others: he rectifies with four cards too and
lets 1NT absorb more hands. Whether the completion is forcing is disputed
(Martel F1; Fauché–Mazères NF; Bessis undecided).

**What 1NT shows.** Responder's 1NT is unchanged: a good stopper, 8–10H
(Martel; SEF 2024 also moves the minor-opening 1NT to 8/10 HL per
[scjc-bridge.fr](https://scjc-bridge.fr/les-nouveautes-du-sef-2018)). Opener's
1NT rebid is a first-zone balanced hand that may still hold three cards in
responder's major.

**Evidence limits.** No English-language Cachalot source was found. The
Voldoire text, the Bridge Academy course, and the Le Bridgeur replay page
were not retrievable. The Bessis text is a transcription, and Martel's
tables are her reading of Bridge Academy, not Bessis's own figures.
