# A quotation for the T-Walsh introduction

Research checked 9 October 2026.

## Recommended quotation

> the 1♦ response to 1♣ is severely underused.

— Richard Granville, [“Is Fantunes still a good bidding system?”](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/is-fantunes-still-a-good-bidding-system/),
18 September 2015, in the discussion immediately following the initial response
table. This is an exact excerpt from the author's own article.

Granville explicitly applies this argument to standard systems such as 2/1:
Walsh gives priority to majors on hands below game-going strength, leaving the
cheapest response underused. He then explains that moving diamond hands to
1♠ or higher gives up little while freeing a useful extra step for major-suit
auctions. His contrasting assessment of the forcing Fantunes club is separate
and should not be imported into Muscat's introduction.
[Source](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/is-fantunes-still-a-good-bidding-system/).

This is a particularly close match to the requested progression: popular Walsh
practice, an underused 1♦ response, then rotating the responses. “Underused”
is more accurate than “useless.” The source supports popularity, not a measured
claim that Walsh is universal in modern bidding.

## Supporting background

Bob Heitzman's [“Low Level Bridge: The Utility 1 Diamond Response”](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/low-level-bridge-the-utility-1-diamond-response/),
14 December 2011, opens by describing Walsh as the expert practice of the day.
His explanation confirms that weaker responders bypass diamonds for a four-card
major, allowing balanced openers to bypass their own majors after 1♣–1♦.
The article then proposes a different method; use its opening only as background
for Walsh, not as an endorsement of transfers.

Ray Green's [“Transfer Walsh – transfer responses to 1C”](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html),
revised February 2016, directly supports the rotation to 1♦ showing hearts and
1♥ showing spades, with 1♠ available as a relay or transfer to notrump. His
worked version uses a no-four-card-major 1♠, unlike variants that require
diamonds. This makes it useful support for Muscat's particular response map.
