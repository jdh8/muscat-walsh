# Transfer Walsh minor-opening upper limits: 20 or 21?

Checked 30 October 2026 against the cited system documents. The relevant
distinction is **ordinary unbalanced minor openings versus balanced hands**.
An 18–20 balanced rebid does not establish a 20-point ceiling for unbalanced
hands.

## Direct evidence

| System | Ordinary 1♣ / 1♦ maximum | Strong-hand treatment and qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Michael Goetze, *2/1 T-Walsh*, pre-0.2 | **21 points** for both unbalanced minors | Opening table explicitly gives 11–21. Balanced 1♣ types are 11–13 and 17–19. Introduction assigns balanced 20+ HCP and unbalanced hands worth 22+ points to partnership-chosen two-level openings. |
| Mairead Haugh–Roisin de Jong, IBU Women's Trials 2025 | **20** for both unbalanced minors | Both opening rows say balanced 12–19 points / unbalanced 11–20. They play a two-card club, four-card diamond and transfer responses over 1♣. |
| Lola Mingot–Maria Panadero, Spanish federation-hosted card | **21 HCP** for both minors | Both rows explicitly say natural, 3+ cards, 11–21 HCP. The club responses identify Transfer Walsh / Cachalote. |

Sources:

- [Goetze, printed p. 3, and introduction p. 1](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=3).
  The opening table uses “points”; it should not be rewritten as an exclusively
  HCP-based rule. Section 2.1.6 does separately describe its 19–21 strong raises
  in HCP terms with shortness and suitable honor location.
- [Haugh–de Jong, PDF p. 2](https://www.cbai.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Haugh-De-Jong-2025.pdf#page=2).
  Their 2♣ row says game-forcing unbalanced or 23+ balanced; 2NT is 20–21
  balanced. This is explicit evidence for the printed 20 ceiling, but the card
  does not settle every borderline 21-point unbalanced hand. It is not evidence
  that every 21 HCP automatically satisfies their game-force criterion.
- [Mingot–Panadero, opening table](https://www.aebridge.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/System%20Lola%20Mingot%20-%20Maria%20Panadero.pdf).
  This is a three-card-minor system, so it supplies a numerical precedent rather
  than an exact match for Muscat's minor-length requirements.

These examples establish that **both ceilings exist**. This small source
comparison does not establish which is more prevalent.

## Relevant references that do not establish a minor-opening ceiling

**Ron Beall's Toucan Club (2020):** the Goldstone–Beall convention card on
printed p. 30 lists strong, game-forcing 2♣ as 21+ HCP and 2NT as 20–21.
However, its minor-opening box has no numerical upper limit, and the prose
on p. 22 leaves the strong 2♣ structure to the partnership. A 20 ceiling is
a plausible inference for that card's ordinary unbalanced openings, not an
explicitly stated universal Toucan rule. The card was visually checked because
it is an embedded image.
[Author's free book, PDF p. 32](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf#page=32).

**Danny Molenaar–Tim Verbeek:** the NBB card explicitly gives 18–20 for the
1NT rebid after a major transfer. Its 1♣ and 1♦ rows do not state a numerical
unbalanced maximum. Therefore, its balanced ladder cannot establish whether
the natural minor openings stop at 20 or 21. This is especially relevant
because Muscat cites this pair for its balanced ladder.
[NBB partnership card](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf).

## Natural 2NT openings

These are opening bids, not 2NT rebids or responses.

| System | Natural 2NT range | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haugh–de Jong | **20–21 balanced** | Explicit in the [opening table, PDF p. 3](https://www.cbai.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Haugh-De-Jong-2025.pdf#page=3). |
| Goetze | **20–21 balanced, suggested** | Refers to 2NT or whichever opening shows 20–21; the two-level scheme is left to the partnership. [Notes, p. 3](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=3). |
| Mingot–Panadero | **20–21 or 20–22: inconsistent** | The summary on p. 1 says 20–21; the opening table on p. 2 says 20–22. Neither can be selected confidently from this [card](https://www.aebridge.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/System%20Lola%20Mingot%20-%20Maria%20Panadero.pdf) alone. |
| Molenaar–Verbeek | **20+–23−, strong balanced** | Exact notation in the [NBB card](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf), visually checked. It also lists 22–24 balanced under 2♣, so the descriptions overlap. |

For Muscat, **21–22 balanced** would be a straightforward next range after
the existing 18–20 balanced rebid. This is a design inference, not an adopted
agreement, and does not determine the maximum for unbalanced minor openings.

## Implication for Muscat

Current [openings](../src/Openings.md) cap both minors at 20 and route 18–20
balanced through 1♣. Both [red-transfer rebids](../src/1C/1R.md) and
[no-major continuations](../src/1C/1S.md) also cap the strong unbalanced
descriptions at 20.

The following is a design recommendation, not a source agreement:

- Keep **18–20 balanced** as its own range if that is the intended notrump
  ladder; changing the unbalanced maximum need not change it.
- Use **11–21 unbalanced, excluding hands that qualify for the strong opening**
  if ordinary non-game-forcing 21-counts should start naturally. Goetze gives a
  clear precedent for including 21 at the one level.
- Retain **11–20** if the strong-opening structure deliberately covers the
  remaining 21-point unbalanced hands. Haugh–de Jong gives a direct precedent
  for that published ceiling.

The decision is therefore about the boundary with the strong opening, not a
requirement of Transfer Walsh. A change to 21 would also require consistent
edits to the affected unbalanced rebids; changing only the opening summary
would leave contradictory ranges. No system files were changed by this
research.
