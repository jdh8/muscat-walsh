# Responder's major rebid after a Transfer Walsh reverse

Checked 12 October 2026. These are documented examples, not evidence of a
universal Transfer Walsh agreement. `M` is the major shown by the initial
transfer; lengths refer to responder's major, not opener's reverse suit.

## A confirmed five-card treatment: Ash–Bayer

[Ash–Bayer system notes, §§3.1.1–2, printed pp. 9–10](https://www.dubiouslogic.com/bridge/system/macc/docs/MA_AB-Jan-2017%202.pdf#page=9)
explicitly make responder's own-major rebid a **one-round force showing 5+**,
without requiring extra values. The document's title says October 2016;
its filename says January 2017. The PDF downloaded successfully even though
the web reader timed out.

| Auction | Documented treatment |
| --- | --- |
| 1♣–1♦; 2♦–2♥ | F1, 5+ hearts; 2♠ is the weak Blackout signal |
| 1♣–1♥; 2♦–2♠ | F1, 5+ spades, moderate values; weak five-card hands prefer the lower 2♥ Blackout |

With game-forcing strength and five cards plus a minor fit, responder normally
shows that fit directly. Crucially, opener's reverse **can retain three-card
major support**: the continuations expressly include 1345/3145 shapes, where
shape is spades–hearts–diamonds–clubs. This is a useful five-card precedent,
but has a different support inference from Muscat.
[Source, pp. 9–10](https://www.dubiouslogic.com/bridge/system/macc/docs/MA_AB-Jan-2017%202.pdf#page=9).

## Other manuscripts: do not invent an unstated length

- **Michael Goetze, 2/1 T-Walsh, pre-0.2, §2.1.7, pp. 8–9:** natural
  reverses use Ingberman: the lower of 2NT and fourth suit shows weakness,
  with fourth suit determined by suits *shown*. Thus
  1♣–1♥; 2♦–2♥ is artificial Ingberman. Other continuations are natural and
  forcing, but this section does **not state 5+ or 6+** for responder's major
  rebid. The 2♦ and 2♥ reverses can start at 16 points; the spade jump reverse
  requires 18+.
  [Authored PDF, §2.1.7](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=8).
- **Marc Umeno–Josh Sher, Supernatural, §6.12:** responder's major rebid is
  F1 and may pass opener's next minimum bid. A jump to 3M shows 10–12 points;
  other bids outside the listed exceptions are natural and GF. **No major
  length is stated in this general reverse section.** The manuscript does
  explicitly specify 5+ spades after the *natural* auction
  1♦–1♠; 2♥–2♠ (§6.4.2), but that is not proof of a transferred-major
  agreement. Inspected the previously downloaded original manuscript text;
  current web fetch failed.
  [Original manuscript](https://bridgewithdan.com/supernatural26april03/).
- **Fallenius–Welland, 2002, p. 3:** after 1♣–1♦; 2♦, responder's 2M or
  other-major/fourth-suit continuation allows a non-GF auction. The notes
  **do not specify the major rebid length** there. The same principles are
  stated to apply after the spade transfer.
  [Authored notes, p. 3](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf#page=3).
- **Bruce Watson, 12 December 2025, pp. 6–7:** labels opener's 2♦/2♥
  rebids standard reverses with longer clubs but supplies **no responder
  continuation or major length** for these auctions. Downloaded and inspected
  the current author-hosted PDF.
  [Authored notes](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf#page=6).
- **Ron Beall, Toucan Club, free 2020 notes:** no explicit responder-major
  length after a reverse was found in the 37-page document. It cannot support
  a claim that Toucan uses 6+ here.
  [Free authored notes](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf).

No explicit **6+ reverse-specific** agreement was confirmed in the inspected
primary sources. General descriptions of six-card rebids after *transfer
completion* concern a different auction and do not establish this agreement.

## Does opener's reverse deny three-card support?

Rechecked primary sources on 12 October 2026. **Transfer Walsh by itself does
not establish a denial.** Completion showing three cards is only one direction
of an implication; whether *every* three-card hand must complete depends on
strength and the rest of the rebid structure. The table distinguishes direct
statements, deductions from routing, and missing agreements. Here `M` is
responder's *shown* major, not the artificial red response.

Goetze provides the clearest routing illustration: a strong 1345 hand over
the heart transfer has three hearts but five clubs, so cannot use the 6+♣
three-card 2NT rebid; the table's natural 16+ 2♦ is available. Likewise,
18+ 4315 over the heart transfer fits the natural 2♠ reverse. These are
**our deductions from his tables**, not example hands printed by the author.
[Goetze, pp. 4–5, 8](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf#page=4).

**1♣–1♥; 1♠**, not a natural 1M opening. Numerical ranges below are
reported only where stated or expressly marked as deductions. “Minimum”
alone does not establish an exact 14-, 15-, or 17-point ceiling.

The sharp comparison is **Goetze's explicit 15-point unbalanced ceiling
versus the Molenaar–Verbeek article's explicit 17-point ceiling**. Ash–Bayer
and Fallenius–Welland do not furnish comparably precise ceilings. Watson
and Toucan emphasize support count and have materially different strong-hand
routing; giving all these systems the same “minimum = 11–15” meaning would
be unsupported.

## Application to Muscat: a design judgment

In the current [`src/1C/1R.md`](../src/1C/1R.md), all these reverses promise
at most two cards in responder's major. A five-card rebid cannot uncover an
eight-card fit; a six-card rebid can uncover a 6–2 fit. This makes **6+ a
reasonable default for a natural GF major rebid in Muscat**: direct 2♠ after
1♣–1♥; 2♦, and natural 3♥/3♠ after a reverse. A 5+ descriptive 2♠ remains
defensible if the partnership wants that information, but it is not an
invitation to discover a 5–3 fit. This is a recommendation based on Muscat's support inference,
not a documented Transfer Walsh consensus.

Muscat's cheapest-step Blackout must remain distinct: 1♣–1♦; 2♦–2♥ and
1♣–1♥; 2♥–2♠ are artificial weakness signals and promise no major length.
Five-card GF hands need a route through a natural minor bid, natural 2NT,
or another agreed forcing continuation. Rebidding six cards still does not
guarantee a fit: opener may have a singleton or void, so the rebid should
describe the suit rather than irrevocably set trumps.
