# Write-ups of systems with T-Walsh responses to 1♣

Research checked 8 October 2026. The assessments below concern usefulness for
Muscat Walsh: natural five-card majors, a two-card club, four-card diamonds,
and 1♣–1♦/1♥ showing hearts/spades. In particular, a no-major 1♠ catchall and a
diamond-showing 1♠ are different agreements.

## Filtered shortlist: minimum balanced hands accept with two cards

Preference: after 1♣–1♦/1♥, completing to 1♥/1♠ may show a minimum
balanced hand with only two cards; an unbalanced completion needs support.
The 1NT rebid should show a stronger balanced hand.

**Molenaar–Verbeek is the closest additional range match.** Goetze and Ray
Green remain useful; Supernatural and Gibson also use minimum balanced
completion, but their strong 1NT starts at 17. Fallenius–Welland is a substantial
reference with an explicit singleton judgment exception. The additional
source profiles below distinguish full manuscripts from cards and articles.

Goetze and Green show four-card support separately. Green also matches the no-major 1♠
response; Goetze's 1♠ instead promises clubs. Goetze offers more extensive
continuations, while Green is the closer starting point for Muscat's response
allocation. These are matches for the rebid principle, not exact matches for
the proposed strength ranges. [Goetze](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf),
[Green](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html).

Exclude Toucan and Watson from this shortlist: completion promises exactly
three-card support, so a minimum balanced hand with two cards does not
complete. Poe remains unverified against this criterion because the publisher's
sample ends before the detailed response/rebid chapters. The source profiles
below retain the earlier research for comparison.
[Toucan](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf),
[Watson](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf),
[Poe sample](https://ebooksbridge.com/uploads/files/products/samples/743/SAMPLEHONORS_TransferResponsesToOneClubWithRelays.pdf).

## Danny Molenaar–Tim Verbeek — partnership system

[Primary NBB convention card, MK2526 directory](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf).
[Guus Schreiber's interview-based explanation, IMP July/August 2025, printed pp. 30–32](https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/v3/95b379df2f94d1433ff82969af2dfd35c83e1535.pdf#page=30).

The pair's card confirms minimum balanced transfer acceptance and an **18–20
1NT rebid**. The Dutch article explains 1♣–1♦–1♥ as 12–14 balanced or 11–17
unbalanced with three hearts, versus 1NT=18–19 balanced. Four-card minimum
support uses 2♥. Both completions and strong 1NT rebids lead to two-way
checkback. Their 1♠ response denies a four-card major, making this especially
relevant to Muscat. This is a named partnership's developed system, but the
accessible materials are a card and selected explanatory agreements, not its
full system book. The card's 18–20 and article's 18–19 should not be silently
combined. The old bridge.nl download address returned 404. The NBB
February 2026 event page links the same file on bridgebond.nl; that address
was downloaded successfully on 8 October 2026. [Card](https://www.bridgebond.nl/download.php?file=Wedstrijdzaken%2FSysteemkaarten%2FMK2526%2FNETH_OPEN_Molenaar_Verbeek+version+without+convention+names.pdf),
[article](https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/v3/95b379df2f94d1433ff82969af2dfd35c83e1535.pdf#page=30).

In the article's version, 2NT opens 20–21, 2♣ includes 22–24 balanced or
game-forcing clubs/hearts/spades, and Multi 2♦ includes 25+ balanced or
game-forcing diamonds. This answers the very-strong-hand question for that
version specifically. [Article](https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/v3/95b379df2f94d1433ff82969af2dfd35c83e1535.pdf#page=30).

## Marc Umeno–Josh Sher — *Supernatural*, 19 March 2003

[Original authored Word manuscript](https://bridgewithdan.com/supernatural26april03/),
linked as Sher–Umeno Supernatural from [Dan Neill's resource directory](https://bridgewithdan.com/resources/).

A substantial full system manuscript, approximately 63 pages, covering all
openings, competitive bidding, relays and slam methods. Sections 6.3.1–2
explicitly complete major transfers with **11–13 balanced, two or three cards**,
and rebid **1NT with 17–19 balanced without four-card support**. A minimum
unbalanced three-card fit with a singleton instead raises to 2M. Thus it fits
the minimum-balanced acceptance principle, but does not include unbalanced
three-card hands in 1M. Its notrump opening is 14–16; 1♣ normally has three
clubs, but may have two with 17–19 balanced. [Manuscript](https://bridgewithdan.com/supernatural26april03/).

The opening summary gives 2NT as 19+–21. Section 8 specifies
2♣–2♦–2NT=22–23 balanced, while the modified Kokish sequence
2♣–2♦–2♥–2♠–2NT shows a good 24+ balanced hand. The original Word file
was downloaded and its text inspected; subsequent direct fetches sometimes
returned 403. [Manuscript](https://bridgewithdan.com/supernatural26april03/).

## Björn Fallenius–Roy Welland — 2/1 notes, 2002

[26-page authored system notes, archive mirror](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf).

Printed p. 3 gives completion as 11–13 balanced or selected awkward unbalanced
hands with three-card support; 1NT shows 17–19 balanced. There is an explicit
exception allowing the example's majors to be reversed, producing a singleton
in responder's major. It also occasionally completes with a poor four-card
balanced fit. **This is a close structural reference, not a strict guarantee
of three-card support outside minimum balanced hands.** It has substantive
chapters on other openings, competition, notrump continuations and slam
bidding; its final speculative section is expressly unimplemented. The
no-major 1♠ response has a game-forcing diamond/major exception.
[Notes](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf).

1NT opens 14–16; 20–21 balanced starts with Multi 2♦. Strong hands use 2♣,
which also contains a weak diamond two-bid. The notes do not explicitly state
the higher balanced ranges after 2♣; do not infer a documented 22+ threshold.
[Notes, pp. 2 and 12–13](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_fallenius_welland_2-1.pdf).

## Paul Gibson — *Transfer Walsh*, September 2008

[Author's article at The Beer Card](https://thebeercard.blogspot.com/2008/09/transfer-walsh.html).
The public [Blogger feed for that post](https://thebeercard.blogspot.com/feeds/posts/default/22331808067687815?alt=json)
provided its complete text when the normal page did not expose it.

Gibson explicitly credits the Fallenius–Welland notes as his starting point.
His rebid table gives 1M=11–13 balanced with two or three cards, and
1NT=17–19 balanced, possibly with three-card support. With three-card support
the minimum completion has no ruffing values; supported club two-suiters
usually raise instead. His 1♠ response shows diamonds, with a game-forcing
major exception. This is a useful authored explanation and adaptation, not
another independent full-system manuscript. [Article](https://thebeercard.blogspot.com/2008/09/transfer-walsh.html).

## Further candidates checked and excluded

- [BOA Aasan, 2004](https://org.ntnu.no/mareritt/boasys04.pdf): strong balanced
  hands rebid 2NT; the 1NT rebid is weak, so it fails the filter.
- [BridgeTV, Simon de Wijs–Tim Verbeek, v1.12, 3 December 2020](https://www.bridgewijs.nl/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/bridgeTV-systeem-versie-1.12.docx):
  completion promises three cards; 1NT shows 12–14 with at most two. It does
  not share Molenaar–Verbeek's qualifying structure despite the article's
  suggestion to consult the school's materials.
- BruWil is an interesting named precursor, but its detailed rebids were not
  verified from an accessible primary text; do not add it to the confirmed list.

## Michael Goetze — *2/1 T-Walsh*, version pre-0.2

[Readable 18-page PDF](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf).
[Original author URL](https://bridge.mgoetze.net/21twalsh.pdf) failed to fetch
during this check; the club-hosted copy is the author's actual document.

This is a useful free explanatory starting point for a natural
club-or-balanced system. The 1♣ chapter occupies printed pp. 3–11 and explains
transfer completion, responder's next round, strong notrump rebids, natural club
rebids, reverses, raises, diamond hands and no-major hands. It also develops the
other one-level openings; later chapters have unfinished headings. The prose
gives reasons for choices, rather than just a convention-card table.
[Source: PDF](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf).

Its 1NT is 14–16; 1♣ includes 11–13/17–19 balanced and natural club hands.
Completion can show a weak balanced hand with only two-card support, making its
strength ladder relevant. However, **1♠ promises 4+ clubs, 7+ points and no
four-card major**; it is not Muscat's catchall. This is a draft: some later
sections are empty, and it explicitly omits passed-hand bidding and two-level
openings. High confidence in the documented agreements; useful as a design
reference, not a finished system to adopt wholesale. [Source: PDF](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf).

## Ray Green — *Transfer Walsh*, revised February 2016

[First-party club article, “Transfer Walsh – transfer responses to 1C”](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html).
[Four-page PDF copy](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Transfer-Walsh.pdf).

The club article identifies Ray Green as author. It explains the choice between
support-based completion and strength-based completion, then gives a coherent
worked structure. Here **1♠ is a relay strictly denying a four-card major**,
covering balanced hands or a long minor. Minimum balanced hands complete a
major transfer with two or three cards; 1NT shows 17–18 and 2NT 19–20. It gives
responder's continuations, minor invitations, and treatment after interference.
[Source: Green's article](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html).

This is the closest short explanation found to Muscat's no-major 1♠ and strong
1NT-rebid idea. Its distinctive direct responses show both majors: 1NT=5–4,
2♣=4–4 with 11+, 2♦=5–5. Thus its continuations depend on a different allocation
of hands. It is a substantial 1♣ subsystem, not full system notes; the author
offers fuller notes by email. High confidence in attribution and agreements.
[Source: Green's article](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html).

## Bruce Watson — *Standard with Transfer Responses to 1♣*, December 2025 revision

[Author-hosted 37-page PDF](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf).

This supplies detailed auction tables: opening structure and first responses,
the 1♣ continuations on printed pp. 5–8, other openings, and extended XYZ.
It uses five-card majors and a 15–17 notrump. **1♠ is a no-four-card-major
catchall**, while 1♦/1♥ show hearts/spades. Completion normally shows exactly
three-card support; four-card support is raised. Responder's later bids extend
transfers through the two level, and a strong 2NT rebid has TARC checkback.
[Source: Watson's PDF](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf).

It is a broad reference for continuations, but its 1♣ is explicitly three-way:
balanced outside the notrump range, natural clubs, **or any strong two-bid**.
That last option can have no clubs and is a material departure from natural
5542. Watson credits Lyle Poe's relay structure, Polish Club and T-Walsh;
relays are optional. High confidence in the PDF's agreements. The initial
fetch succeeded, but some subsequent web fetches timed out. Prefer this dated
version over the earlier 12 May “SOS” notes when comparing the same author's
material. [Source: Watson's PDF](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf).

## Ron Beall — *Toucan Club* (2020); expanded book with Ron Woodard

[Free 37-page system PDF](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf).
[Author's introduction at The Bridge World](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/esoterica/toucan.html).
[Publisher: *Toucan Club Complete*](https://masterpointpress.com/toucan-club-complete).

The free version describes a natural, nonforcing 2+ club and an unbalanced
diamond within a 2/1 framework. Printed pp. 7–14 cover the 1♣ structure; later
chapters cover other openings and competition. It is a readable, developed
system reference. [Source: free PDF](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf).

Transfer completion shows **exactly three-card support**, with a minimum
four-card raise at the two level. **1♠ suggests 1NT**, but is not an absolute
denial of majors: game-forcing 5–5 majors can start there. Transfers to both
minors are also included: 1NT shows clubs and 2♣ diamonds. Its natural opening
framework is close to Muscat, but its support-based rebids differ from Muscat's
strength ladder. High confidence in the free version. The expanded book is a
separate purchase; this research did not inspect its full text.
[Source: Beall's article](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/esoterica/toucan.html).

## Lyle Poe — *Transfer Responses to One Club with Relays* (2019)

[Publisher's sample PDF](https://ebooksbridge.com/uploads/files/products/samples/743/SAMPLEHONORS_TransferResponsesToOneClubWithRelays.pdf).

A substantial book option: the contents place transfer responses at p. 9,
opener/responder rebids at p. 21, competition after 1♣ at p. 73, and relays at
p. 109. The sample includes explanations and example hands rather than only
auction tables. However, **1♣ is forcing and includes any strong two-bid**;
2♣ is natural, usually six clubs, 8–14 HCP. The nominal two-card club can
therefore be shorter in the strong variant. This is a major opening-structure
departure from Muscat, similar to Watson's adaptation. High confidence in the
sample and contents; the sample ends before the response chapter, so the exact
1♠ definition and detailed continuations were not independently checked.
[Source: publisher's sample](https://ebooksbridge.com/uploads/files/products/samples/743/SAMPLEHONORS_TransferResponsesToOneClubWithRelays.pdf).

## Ranges and very strong hands: Goetze and Watson

Goetze's balanced ladder is 11–13 in 1♣, 14–16 opening 1NT, 17–19 in 1♣
(rebid 1NT after a major transfer), and 20+ through partnership-chosen
two-level openings. His ordinary suit openings extend to 21 points;
game-forcing unbalanced hands, described as worth 22+ **points**, go through
the chosen strong opening. He does not specify the full two-level scheme.
Thus a 21-point unbalanced hand can still begin one of a suit; a 21-HCP
balanced hand uses the strong balanced opening. See printed pp. 1, 3, 5.
[Source: Goetze's PDF](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/T-Walsh.pdf).

Watson's currently downloadable PDF is dated **12 December 2025**, although
the web index returned a May 2025 copy. Its balanced ladder is 12–14 in 1♣,
15–17 opening 1NT, 18–19 in 1♣, and 20–21 opening 2NT. Ordinary unbalanced
minor openings cap at 19 HCP, majors at 10–19. Any strong two-bid instead
opens the forcing 1♣. After 1♦/1♥/1♠, opener's 2♣ rebid identifies that
strong hand and asks for controls; completion denies it. See pp. 2, 5, 7.
The notes do not state an exact HCP threshold for “strong two-bid.” They
give the strong-hand route but do not separately define every marginal
20–21 unbalanced hand. In competition, rebid 2♣ if available; otherwise
double for takeout, cue-bid or bid game (p. 22).
[Source: Watson's current PDF](https://www.math.mun.ca/~bruce2/1C_with_extended_transfers.pdf).

## A short diamond-response variant for comparison

[Gerben's *Transfer Walsh* page](https://www.geocities.ws/gerben42/twalsh.html)
describes **1♠=5+ diamonds**, denying a major except on strong hands, and
completion with exactly three-card major support. It also records an
alternative minor-sorting scheme attributed to Micha Keijzers: 1♠ is balanced
6–12 or diamonds 10+, with possible four-card major on a game force. This is
useful for understanding why “T-Walsh” does not by itself determine the meaning
of 1♠. It is brief, not a full system, and neither scheme is Muscat's exact
no-major catchall. [Source: personal system page](https://www.geocities.ws/gerben42/twalsh.html).

## Existing reference: *Polish Club 2020: Expert*

The book already listed under Transfer Walsh in `src/README.md` uses a negative
1♦ response and natural 1♥/1♠ responses in its uncontested 1♣ structure
(printed p. 32). It is useful background for other system choices, rather than
an example of T-Walsh responses to 1♣.
[Source: publisher's sample](https://masterpointpress.com/uploads/files/products/samples/782/SAMPLE_HONORS_PolishClub2020Expert.pdf).

## Additional range checks: Green, Toucan and Poe

- **Ray Green:** 1NT opens 15–16. After a major transfer, minimum balanced
  12–14 completes; 17–18 rebids 1NT and 19–20 rebids 2NT. Balanced 21–22
  opens 2NT; 23–24 starts 2♣ then 2NT. The separate notrump-ladder article
  assigns 25+ to 2♣ then 3NT. No numerical threshold or detailed continuation
  for very strong unbalanced hands is supplied in these articles.
  [Source: Green's club articles](https://www.bridgewebs.com/brunton/page41.html).
- **Toucan (2020):** 1NT 15–17; the 1♣ strong balanced rebid is 18–19. The
  authors' convention card on printed p. 30 gives 2NT 20–21 and strong 2♣
  21+, game forcing, with a waiting 2♦ response. Thus 21 balanced fits the
  direct 2NT range, while very strong unbalanced hands use strong 2♣. The
  prose on p. 22 explicitly leaves the 2♣ continuations to the partnership;
  a complete higher balanced ladder is not specified. The convention card
  was visually checked because its contents are an embedded image.
  [Source: Toucan PDF, pp. 22, 30](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/FREE_ToucanClub1.pdf).
- **Poe (2019):** 1♣ includes 11–14 balanced, 18–19 balanced, appropriate
  natural clubs and any strong two-bid. 1NT opens 15–17; 2NT opens 20–21.
  Very strong unbalanced hands start forcing 1♣. The sample does not give
  a numerical strong-two-bid threshold or the later strong rebids; those
  belong to the chapter beginning p. 39. Strong 2♣ is unavailable because
  that opening is natural, usually 6+ clubs, 8–14.
  [Source: Poe sample, pp. 1–5 and contents](https://ebooksbridge.com/uploads/files/products/samples/743/SAMPLEHONORS_TransferResponsesToOneClubWithRelays.pdf).
